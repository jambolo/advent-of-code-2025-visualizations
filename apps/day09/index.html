Day 9: Movie Theater

You slide down the firepole in the corner of the playground and land in the North Pole base movie theater!

The movie theater has a big tile floor with an interesting pattern. Elves here are redecorating the theater by switching out some of the square tiles in the big grid they form. Some of the tiles are red; the Elves would like to find the largest rectangle that uses red tiles for two of its opposite corners.

How the Solution Works

Overview

The solver finds the largest axis-aligned rectangle with red corners that lies entirely within the polygon formed by red and green tiles.

Algorithm Steps

  1. Parse corners: Load all 496 red tile coordinates from the input file.
  2. Build edges: Create edges connecting consecutive corners (including wrapping from last to first) to form a closed polygon.
  3. Normalize edges: Order each edge's vertices so the smaller coordinate comes first.
  4. Generate candidates: For every pair of corners (i, j), treat them as opposite corners of a potential rectangle.
  5. Part 2 containment check:
    • Verify no polygon edge crosses through the rectangle's interior
    • Use ray-casting: cast a ray from the rectangle's interior and count how many horizontal edges it crosses
    • Odd crossings = inside the polygon (valid)
  6. Track maximum: Keep the rectangle with the largest area that passes all checks.

Key Functions

  • crosses() - Checks if a polygon edge intersects the rectangle's boundary
  • fully_contained() - Combines edge-crossing check with ray-cast interior test
  • consecutive_pairs() - Iterates through adjacent corners including wrap-around

Complexity

With N corners, there are N×(N-1)/2 candidate pairs to check. For 496 corners, that's 122,760 potential rectangles. The containment check is O(E) where E is the number of edges.
