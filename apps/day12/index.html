Day 12: Christmas Tree Farm

You're almost out of time, but there can't be much left to decorate. Although there are no stairs, elevators, escalators, tunnels, chutes, teleporters, firepoles, or conduits here that would take you deeper into the North Pole base, there is a ventilation duct. You jump in.

After bumping around for a few minutes, you emerge into a large, well-lit cavern full of Christmas trees!

There are a few Elves here frantically decorating before the deadline. They think they'll be able to finish most of the work, but the one thing they're worried about is the presents for all the young Elves that live here at the North Pole. It's an ancient tradition to put the presents under the trees, but the Elves are worried they won't fit.

The Problem

The presents come in 6 standard shapes (each fits in a 3x3 grid). Each tree region has specific dimensions and requires certain numbers of each present shape to fit underneath.

Presents can be rotated and flipped, but cannot overlap (the # cells cannot share space). The empty cells (.) in a shape's diagram do not block other presents.

How the Solution Works

Overview

The solver uses a heuristic two-pass approach that can quickly classify most regions without solving the full bin-packing problem.

Step 1: Area-Based Rejection

Calculate the total area of all required presents. If the total present area exceeds the region area, the presents cannot possibly fit and the region is immediately rejected.

Region: 4x4 = 16 cells Presents: 2x shape4 (7 cells each) = 14 cells 14 ≤ 16, so area check passes

Step 2: Slot-Based Acceptance

Since all shapes fit within a 3x3 bounding box, count how many non-overlapping 3x3 slots exist in the region. If the number of presents ≤ number of slots, each present is guaranteed a home.

Region: 12x6 → (12÷3) × (6÷3) = 4 × 2 = 8 slots Presents: 6 total 6 ≤ 8 slots → Accepted!

Step 3: Undetermined

Regions that pass the area check but fail the slot check remain undetermined. These would require a full backtracking search to resolve, which the solver doesn't attempt.

Why This Works

  • Most regions either clearly don't have enough space (rejected) or have plenty of room (accepted)
  • The heuristic avoids expensive NP-hard bin packing for obvious cases
  • For this puzzle's input, the heuristics are sufficient to classify all needed regions
